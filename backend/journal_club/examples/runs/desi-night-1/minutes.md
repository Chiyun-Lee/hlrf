## Overview

Taken together, these papers establish that BAO is a robust geometric probe. desi2024iii measures it to 0.52% across six bins, desi2024iv adds a 2% Lyman-alpha measurement at z = 2.33, and planck2018vi and eboss2020 show that earlier BAO, CMB and supernova data fit a cosmological constant. The new result is desi2024vi, where DESI with CMB prefers w0 > -1 and wa < 0 at 2.6 sigma, rising to 3.9 sigma with DES-SN5YR (dessn5yr2024). The disputes were mostly ruled against reading this at face value. The Planck and eBOSS results were judged not like-for-like with DESI's, because they use older BAO data or fit a constant w. efstathiou2024 showed that Pantheon+ alone gives no support for evolution and that recalibrating DES-SN5YR's low-redshift supernovae removes its preference, which limits DESI's claim that the supernova sets are consistent. lewis2024's null-energy-condition argument is conditional on that condition and on geometry alone, so DESI's caution stands. None of the papers can say why DESI's z = 0.71 bin differs from eBOSS by 2.5-3 sigma, which supernova bias corrections are correct, or whether the systematics are excluded. The ranked directions point to cross-calibrated low-redshift supernova reanalyses, whether the z < 0.8 offset persists in the next DESI release, and NEC-aware interpretation in place of CPL contours.

- **Agreed:** DESI DR1 BAO and its Lyman-alpha companion deliver precise distance measurements that improve on SDSS, with systematic budgets stated as small relative to statistics.
  > the combined precision of the BAO measurements across the six different redshift bins is ∼0.52%, marking a 1.2-fold improvement over the previous state-of-the-art results using only first-year data (desi2024iii, Abstract)
  > The statistical uncertainties on the BAO parameters from just one year of DESI observations are already smaller than the ones from dMdB20, who used 10 years of BOSS and eBOSS observations. (desi2024iv, Sec. 8)
- **Agreed:** Pantheon+ with Planck-based fits, and older SDSS BAO with Planck, show no support for time-varying dark energy.
  > There is no improvement in χ2 compared to ΛCDM if w0 and wa are allowed to vary. The Pantheon+ SN data therefore offer no support for models with a time varying equation-of-state. (efstathiou2024, Sec. 2)
  > The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data. (eboss2020, Sec. VII, p. 31)

## Consensus

- desi2024iii and dessn5yr2024: Both find mild, low-significance departures from a Planck-like LCDM expansion history at low to intermediate redshift, but neither claims evolving dark energy on its own.
  > We find our measurements are systematically larger than the prediction of the Planck 2018-ΛCDM at z <0.8. (desi2024iii, Abstract)
  > Fitting DES-SN5YR alone to the Flat- w0waCDM model gives an equation of state that is slightly over 2σ from a cosmological constant, marginally preferring a time-varying dark energy (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 4.1.4)
- desi2024iii and eboss2020: Both report a roughly 3 sigma outlier in a SDSS/eBOSS measurement relative to a LCDM expectation or to DESI, but they do not directly contradict or support each other on the same question, so this is only a weak agreement.
  > Accordingly, we found a large discrepancy between the DESI BAO measurement at zeff = 0.71 and the SDSS measurement at zeff = 0.7. (desi2024iii, Sec. 9)
  > After accounting for the covariance between BAO and RSD measurements, the measurement differs from the ΛCDM prediction by 3.0 standard deviations. (eboss2020, Sec. VI.A, p. 26)
- desi2024iii and lewis2024: Both describe DESI DR1 BAO points deviating from Planck LCDM in the lower-distance direction at z<~1, which Lewis shows lies in the NEC-excluded region.
  > We find our measurements are systematically larger than the prediction of the Planck 2018-ΛCDM at z <0.8. (desi2024iii, Abstract)
  > For three of the redshift bins – LRG2, LRG3+ELG2, and ELG2 – the data point centres lie within the exclusion region. (lewis2024, Sec. IV)
- desi2024iv and eboss2020: Both treat Lyman-alpha BAO as a distance probe at z>2 that DESI improves on SDSS/eBOSS; this is weak support and does not address dark-energy evolution directly.
  > The statistical uncertainties on the BAO parameters from just one year of DESI observations are already smaller than the ones from dMdB20, who used 10 years of BOSS and eBOSS observations. (desi2024iv, Sec. 8)
  > The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data. (eboss2020, Sec. VII, p. 31)
- desi2024vi and dessn5yr2024: Both papers find a mild preference for evolving, thawing-like dark energy (w0 > -1) beyond a cosmological constant. DES-SN5YR reaches about 2 sigma alone, and DESI reaches 2.5 to 3.9 sigma in combination, so the DES result weakly supports DESI's.
  > combinations of DESI with CMB or with type Ia supernovae (SN Ia) individually prefer w0 > −1 and wa < 0 (desi2024vi, Abstract)
  > Fitting DES-SN5YR alone to the Flat- w0waCDM model gives an equation of state that is slightly over 2σ from a cosmological constant, marginally preferring a time-varying dark energy (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 4.1.4)
- dessn5yr2024 and eboss2020: Both find the dark-energy equation of state close to w = -1 and compatible with LCDM, with at most a mild SN-only pull toward w > -1 in DES. The datasets differ, so this is weak agreement and not a contradiction.
  > Our best fit equation of state parameter is slightly (more than 1 σ) higher than the cosmological constant value of w = −1 (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 5.1.2)
  > The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data. (eboss2020, Sec. VII, p. 31)
- dessn5yr2024 and lewis2024: Both papers cast doubt on reading the evolving dark energy preference at face value. DES says its w > -1 pull depends on the low-z sample, which has the least systematic control. Lewis says the BAO (w0, wa) contours add no consistent information, so any thawing pull must come from low-z supernovae. This is a weak agreement.
  > it is important to recognize that (a) the low- z sample is the one for which we have the least systematic control (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 5.1.2)
  > The constraint contours in the CPL plane therefore have no consistent interpretation in terms of additional information from BAO (lewis2024, Sec. IV)
- dessn5yr2024 and planck2018vi: Both find a constant dark-energy equation of state near w = -1 (DES only slightly above by about 1 sigma, Planck+SN+BAO consistent with Lambda), so neither gives strong evidence for evolving dark energy.
  > Our best fit equation of state parameter is slightly (more than 1 σ) higher than the cosmological constant value of w = −1 (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 5.1.2)
  > Combining Planck data with Pantheon supernovae and BAO data, the equation of state of dark energy is tightly constrained to w0 =−1.03± 0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant. (planck2018vi, Sec. 8, p. 62)
- eboss2020 and efstathiou2024: Both find data consistent with a cosmological constant: eBOSS BAO+Planck and Pantheon+ alone show no support for evolving dark energy.
  > The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data. (eboss2020, Sec. VII, p. 31)
  > The Pantheon+ SN data therefore offer no support for models with a time varying equation-of-state. (efstathiou2024, Sec. 2)
- eboss2020 and planck2018vi: Both find a low H0 (below 68 km/s/Mpc) from CMB or BAO-anchored routes, in tension with the local distance-ladder value.
  > In all cases, the central values remain below H0 = 68 km s−1Mpc−1, and the uncertainties remain at 1 km s−1Mpc−1 or better. (eboss2020, Sec. IV.B, p. 22)
  > The Planck base-ΛCDM cosmology requires a Hubble constant H0 = (67.4± 0.5) km s−1Mpc−1, in substantial 4.4σ tension with the latest local determination by Riess et al. (2019). (planck2018vi, Sec. 8, p. 62)
- efstathiou2024 and lewis2024: Both place the pull toward evolving dark energy in low-redshift supernova calibration, not in BAO geometry. Efstathiou finds a 0.04-0.05 mag offset in the DES5Y low-redshift samples. Lewis finds the CPL contours carry no extra BAO information beyond LCDM.
  > Most of the low redshift surveys are offset by ∼ 0.04−0.05 mag. relative to the more distant DES supernovae. (efstathiou2024, Sec. 3)
  > The constraint contours in the CPL plane therefore have no consistent interpretation in terms of additional information from BAO (lewis2024, Sec. IV)
- efstathiou2024 and planck2018vi: Both find that Planck-based fits with Pantheon-family supernovae give no evidence for a time-varying dark-energy equation of state, so Planck 2018 supports Efstathiou's Planck-LCDM-consistent Pantheon+ result.
  > There is no improvement in χ2 compared to ΛCDM if w0 and wa are allowed to vary. The Pantheon+ SN data therefore offer no support for models with a time varying equation-of-state. (efstathiou2024, Sec. 2)
  > Combining Planck data with Pantheon supernovae and BAO data, the equation of state of dark energy is tightly constrained to w0 =−1.03± 0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant. (planck2018vi, Sec. 8, p. 62)
- lewis2024 and planck2018vi: Both indicate that dark-energy extensions anchored to the CMB acoustic scale do not relieve the H0 tension (Lewis shows NEC-consistent dark energy only worsens it), a weak agreement.
  > this is now sufficient to show that null-energy consistent dark energy can only exacerbate the Hubble tension. (lewis2024, Sec. II.B.1)
  > The Planck base-ΛCDM cosmology requires a Hubble constant H0 = (67.4± 0.5) km s−1Mpc−1, in substantial 4.4σ tension with the latest local determination by Riess et al. (2019). (planck2018vi, Sec. 8, p. 62)

## Shared limitations

- desi2024iii and desi2024vi: Both papers say that unexplained or unexcluded systematics in the data, whether the z~0.7 DESI–SDSS discrepancy or dataset inconsistencies, could still be behind the low-z BAO offset and the evolving dark energy preference.
  > While this 2.5 σ–3σ discrepancy warrants attention, we have not identified any sources of non-statistical discrepancy from both sets of the data. (desi2024iii, Sec. 8.2)
  > It remains important to thoroughly examine unaccounted-for sources of systematic uncertainties or inconsistencies between the different datasets that might be contributing to these results. (desi2024vi, Sec. 8)
- desi2024iv and desi2024vi: Both papers leave residual unquantified systematics (Lya BAO mock offsets and unstudied cross-correlation shifts vs. possible unaccounted systematics behind the dark-energy preference) as an open caveat, though this is a loose match.
  > Because we find similar discrepancies among the two sets of mocks (LyaColore and Saclay), we can not use those offsets to correct the measurements and we hence have to treat them as systematic uncertainties. (desi2024iv, Sec. 6.5)
  > It remains important to thoroughly examine unaccounted-for sources of systematic uncertainties or inconsistencies between the different datasets that might be contributing to these results. (desi2024vi, Sec. 8)
- desi2024iv and planck2018vi: Both note that Lyα BAO at z~2.3-2.4 is limited by large/noisy uncertainties relative to galaxy BAO, restricting its constraining power.
  > in DESI DR1 the majority of quasars have only been observed once and are therefore on average noisier than in SDSS DR16. (desi2024iv, Sec. 7.2)
  > The errors on these high-redshift BAO measurements are still quite large in comparison with the galaxy measurements and so we do not include them in our default BAO compilation. (planck2018vi, Sec. 5.1, p. 23)

## Directions

1. **Evolving-dark-energy claims will move from quoting DESI+CMB+SN significances with the DES5Y compilation as-is to reporting them after a cross-calibrated, common-pipeline reanalysis of the low-z SN anchor, with the DES5Y-driven 3.9 sigma shrinking toward the Pantheon+ level.** (confidence 0.60)
   from headline significance from SN compilations with independent, simulation-based bias corrections to significance after a joint Pantheon+/Union3/DES5Y low-z recalibration and bias-correction intercomparison, because DES5Y itself says its low-z sample has the least systematic control and its w>-1 pull weakens without it, while Efstathiou finds a 0.04 mag low-z offset tied to bias corrections that removes the preference.; papers: desi2024vi, dessn5yr2024
   > it is important to recognize that (a) the low- z sample is the one for which we have the least systematic control (dessn5yr2024, Sec. 5.1.2)
   > It remains important to thoroughly examine unaccounted-for sources of systematic uncertainties or inconsistencies between the different datasets that might be contributing to these results. (desi2024vi, Sec. 8)

2. **The next DESI BAO release will be judged on whether the z<0.8 offset from Planck LCDM and the z~0.7 LRG disagreement with eBOSS persist at higher statistics, moving from a roughly 3 sigma upper-limit quote to a measured, cross-correlation-resolved DESI-vs-SDSS comparison on the overlapping footprint.** (confidence 0.50)
   from DESI-vs-eBOSS z~0.7 discrepancy quoted as an upper limit under an assumed 0.21 cross-correlation to direct overlap-footprint comparison with measured cross-covariance in DESI Y3/Y5, because DESI DR1 found no non-statistical cause for the 2.5-3 sigma discrepancy and notes the cross-correlation coefficient could be much lower, so its significance is not pinned down.; papers: desi2024iii, eboss2020
   > While this 2.5 σ–3σ discrepancy warrants attention, we have not identified any sources of non-statistical discrepancy from both sets of the data. (desi2024iii, Sec. 8.2)
   > Hence, the cross-correlation coefficient could be much lower than the theoretical estimate, suggesting that the observed 3σ discrepancy between SDSS and DESI is likely an upper limit. (desi2024iii, Sec. 8.2)
   > The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data. (eboss2020, Sec. VII, p. 31)

3. **Interpretation of DESI dynamical dark energy will move from CPL (w0, wa) contours to NEC-aware, theta_*-constrained tests in distance space (D_M, D_H, F_AP relative to LCDM) and physically parameterised models, with later papers reporting whether data points fall inside NEC-excluded regions.** (confidence 0.45)
   from CPL (w0, wa) significance as the evidence for evolving dark energy to NEC-consistent physical-model and distance-inequality tests, because DESI cautions that preferred (w0, wa) may map onto quintessence, while Lewis & Chamberlain show CPL contours have no consistent interpretation and that several DESI bins lie in the NEC-forbidden region.; papers: desi2024vi, lewis2024
   > However, we caution against over-interpretation of this result, since at least some of the (w0, wa) models preferred by our analysis could be mapped to quintessence models (desi2024vi, Sec. 5.2)
   > The constraint contours in the CPL plane therefore have no consistent interpretation in terms of additional information from BAO (lewis2024, Sec. IV)
   > For three of the redshift bins – LRG2, LRG3+ELG2, and ELG2 – the data point centres lie within the exclusion region. (lewis2024, Sec. IV)

4. **Because the DESI w0-wa signal cannot come from BAO alone, later work will move from assigning it to the combined DESI+CMB+SN fit to decomposing it into per-probe contributions with the supernova calibration varied, testing whether the SN low-z pull is what drives it.** (confidence 0.45)
   from joint DESI+CMB+SN significance with SN sets assumed consistent to per-probe decomposition with SN low-z calibration marginalised, because DESI says BAO alone cannot break the w0-wa degeneracy and all probes contribute, while Efstathiou finds Pantheon+ gives no support for evolution and Lewis & Chamberlain locate any thawing pull in z<0.2 supernovae.; papers: desi2024vi, efstathiou2024
   > DESI alone does not have sufficient power to break the degeneracy between w0 and wa (desi2024vi, Sec. 5.2)
   > There is no improvement in χ2 compared to ΛCDM if w0 and wa are allowed to vary. The Pantheon+ SN data therefore offer no support for models with a time varying equation-of-state. (efstathiou2024, Sec. 2)
   > All three of the primary BAO, CMB and SN Ia probes contribute partially to this tension and the results including the 3 SN Ia datasets are mutually consistent with each other. (desi2024vi, Sec. 8)

5. **The Lyman-alpha BAO at z~2.3 will move from a noisy, single-visit DR1 measurement combined with SDSS through an approximate cross-covariance to a multi-visit, DESI-pipeline reanalysis that also includes simulation studies of the quasar cross-correlation BAO shift.** (confidence 0.35)
   from DR1 Lya BAO with mock offsets as a 0.5% systematic and approximate DESI+SDSS combination to multi-visit Lya BAO with N-body mocks and cross-correlation shift studies, because DR1 Lya spectra are mostly single-visit and noisier, the cross-correlation that dominates lacks baryon-velocity bias studies, and the SDSS combination was not reanalysed.; papers: desi2024iv, planck2018vi
   > Studies using hydrodynamical simulations have shown that the bias should be small in the Ly α forest auto-correlation [112, 113], but we do not have equivalent studies for the cross-correlation with quasars. (desi2024iv, Sec. 7.4)
   > in DESI DR1 the majority of quasars have only been observed once and are therefore on average noisier than in SDSS DR16. (desi2024iv, Sec. 7.2)
   > The errors on these high-redshift BAO measurements are still quite large in comparison with the galaxy measurements and so we do not include them in our default BAO compilation. (planck2018vi, Sec. 5.1, p. 23)

6. **Systematic budgets for the next BAO release will move from excluding the N_eff fiducial-cosmology bias and post-unblinding catalogue fixes to budgets that include N_eff-dependent template bias, tested against Planck-calibrated LCDM at the new precision.** (confidence 0.30)
   from BAO systematic budget omitting N_eff fiducial bias to budget including N_eff-dependent template bias, because DESI DR1 excludes the N_eff bias from its 0.245% budget and found a completeness-weight error only after unblinding, while Planck constrains N_eff only to 2.99±0.17.; papers: desi2024iii
   > For the fiducial cosmology systematics, we do not include the bias that can be introduced due to an incorrect assumption of Neff in the fitting model. (desi2024iii, Table 13)
   > After the unblinded results were revealed, an error was found in the LSS catalogs related to the completeness weights that are assigned to random points. (desi2024iii, Sec. 2.3)

## Disputes

### desi2024iii vs planck2018vi
- claim: desi2024iii says "We find our measurements are systematically larger than the prediction of the Planck 2018-ΛCDM at z <0.8." but planck2018vi says "Combining Planck data with Pantheon supernovae and BAO data, the equation of state of dark energy is tightly constrained to w0 =−1.03± 0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant." [context: Whether Planck-calibrated flat ΛCDM with a cosmological constant describes late-time BAO distances: DESI DR1 finds z<0.8 BAO scales systematically above the Planck ΛCDM prediction, while Planck+BAO+SNe finds no preference for evolving dark energy.]
- verdict: **contested**
- reason: Planck verified that its w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 rests on CMB geometry plus Pantheon and BOSS-era BAO, and that DESI's z=0.71 bin conflicts with eBOSS at 2.5–3σ with no identified cause, so DESI's low-z offset is unproven until Y3/Y5, and both claims stay linked.
- [desi2024iii] (verified) Planck's w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 rests on BAO that stops at BOSS DR12, with errors far larger than DESI's 0.52% aggregate. Our blinded, unified pipeline finds z<0.8 scales systematically above Planck-ΛCDM. That isn't a contradiction of Planck's data; it's a newer, tighter test of its prediction. Our systematics budget is 0.245%, well below statistical errors, so the offset isn't a systematic. We're careful: the z=0.71 disagreement with eBOSS is likely an upper limit and has no identified non-statistical cause. DESI Y3/Y5 will show whether the low-z offset persists; Planck's "consistent with Λ" can't settle that.
- [planck2018vi] (verified) Agreed that DESI is a tighter test, but a z<0.8 offset from one year of data doesn't overturn the combined constraint. Planck's geometry rests on BAO that Planck itself calls "a robust geometrical test of cosmology," and w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 stays consistent with Λ. DESI also concedes the z=0.71 LRG bin conflicts with eBOSS at 2.5–3σ with no identified cause, and its 3σ figure is likely an upper limit. Until Y3/Y5 data resolve that, a persistent offset is unproven. Planck's own AL and H0 tensions show where I'd look for systematics instead. Planck supports Λ now and awaits DESI's verdict.
- [desi2024iii] (verified) Planck's w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 is a statement about BOSS-era BAO, not a test DESI's data have failed to pass. We agree the eBOSS bin is unexplained, but 2.5–3σ at one redshift doesn't erase a systematic offset across all z<0.8 bins. Our budget is fixed and small, and we state plainly that we found no non-statistical source in either dataset. Planck's own AL>1 at 2.8σ shows its likelihood isn't perfect either. Y3/Y5 decide whether the offset persists; until then, "consistent with Λ" is a pre-DESI result, not a verdict on it.
- [planck2018vi] (verified) DESI says "consistent with Λ" is pre-DESI. Fair, but Planck's w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 is not just BOSS-era BAO; it rests on CMB geometry plus Pantheon, and I don't claim it as a verdict on DESI. The open point is DESI's own: the z=0.71 LRG bin conflicts with eBOSS at 2.5–3σ with no identified cause. A 0.245% budget only covers detected terms and excludes an incorrect Neff assumption. The AL>1 point doesn't cut one way: I flag it as an internal tension, not as evidence for evolving dark energy. Y3/Y5 should decide, and until the offset persists across independent data, Λ remains the supported model.

### desi2024vi vs eboss2020
- claim: desi2024vi says "combinations of DESI with CMB or with type Ia supernovae (SN Ia) individually prefer w0 > −1 and wa < 0" but eboss2020 says "The SDSS BAO measurements demonstrate that the observed cosmic acceleration is best described by a dark energy equation of state that is consistent with a cosmological constant to better than 6% precision when combined with the Planck temperature and polarization data." [context: Whether BAO combined with CMB/SNe favours an evolving dark energy equation of state (DESI, w0 > -1 and wa < 0) or is consistent with a cosmological constant (eBOSS), though the two use different BAO datasets and precision.]
- verdict: **contested**
- reason: Verified turns show the two results aren't like-for-like: eBOSS's older SDSS BAO plus Planck and Pantheon gave wa consistent with a cosmological constant, and DESI concedes that CMB and SN offsets contribute and that systematics aren't excluded, so DESI's w0 > -1, wa < 0 preference survives only with those limits.
- [desi2024vi] (verified) The two papers don't conflict as much as the framing suggests. eBOSS's 6% result is a constant-w constraint from older SDSS BAO plus Planck. DESI's preference appears only when w is allowed to evolve, and DESI says so: a constant w is consistent with -1. DESI's data are also newer and cover more tracers, over 0.1 < z < 4.2. The evolving signal grows with supernovae, at 2.5, 3.5 and 3.9 sigma, and the three compilations agree with each other. DESI does not claim this is settled. It says unaccounted systematics or dataset inconsistencies must be examined. The next BAO release and the supernova re-analyses should decide whether the preference holds.
- [eboss2020] (verified) Agreed the constraints differ, but that is why the eBOSS result matters. Its w0waCDM fit with Planck, SDSS BAO, Pantheon and DES gives wa = -0.32 +0.28/-0.24 and wp = -1.020 +/- 0.029, so evolution was allowed and stayed consistent with a cosmological constant. The 6% figure is not merely a constant-w statement. Also, DESI's signal is not attributable to BAO alone; DESI says all three probes contribute partially. Even DESI cautions systematics are not excluded. eBOSS also found its largest deviation at z~1.48, 3.0 sigma, in a mostly well-behaved sample. The next release should be judged on whether it repeats the SDSS consistency, not only on rising sigma.
- [desi2024vi] (verified) eBOSS's w0waCDM result is real, but it is not a like-for-like test. It uses older SDSS BAO with Pantheon and DES. DESI's Year-1 data span seven bins and add new tracers. When DESI is combined with CMB or SNe, the evolving-w preference appears and grows with the SN sample. The three compilations agree with each other. I accept that all three probes contribute partially and that systematics are not excluded. That is why the next release should be judged on whether the w0 > -1, wa < 0 preference persists with tighter BAO. It should not be judged on whether it repeats SDSS consistency. The disagreement is data-driven, not a framing issue.
- [eboss2020] (verified) Agreed the datasets differ, but "not like-for-like" cuts both ways: SDSS BAO plus Planck, Pantheon and DES allowed evolution and returned wa = -0.32 +0.28/-0.24, consistent with a cosmological constant. DESI concedes that all three probes contribute partially and that systematics are not excluded. So a rising sigma could reflect CMB or SN offsets, not BAO. A fair test of the next release is whether tighter BAO alone moves the result, and whether it keeps the internal consistency SDSS showed. Persistence of w0 > -1, wa < 0 is necessary, not sufficient.
