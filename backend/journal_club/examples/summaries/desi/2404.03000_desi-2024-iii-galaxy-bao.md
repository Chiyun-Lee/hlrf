# Executive summary
This paper asks how well the first year of DESI spectroscopy (DR1) measures the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) standard ruler, and what the resulting distances say about the Planck 2018 LCDM expansion history. The data are over 5.7 million galaxy and quasar redshifts at 0.1 < z < 2.1 (BGS, LRG, ELG and QSO tracers) over about 7,500 square degrees, an effective volume of roughly 18 Gpc^3. The analysis was blinded at the catalogue level, a first for BAO; fiducial fitting and reconstruction choices and the systematic-error budget were fixed on mocks and blinded catalogues before unblinding, with covariance calibration and catalogue bug-fixes allowed afterwards. The pipeline is unified across tracers and across configuration and Fourier space, uses RecSym reconstruction and a spline-based broadband model, and combines LRGs and ELGs at 0.8 < z < 1.1. BAO is detected in all six redshift bins (4.0 sigma for BGS to 9.1 sigma for LRG3+ELG1), with aggregate precision of 0.52%, versus 0.64% for all of SDSS. The systematic budget is 0.245% (isotropic) and 0.3% (anisotropic), well below statistical errors. The headline tension is that the BAO scales at z < 0.8 sit systematically above the Planck LCDM prediction, and the z = 0.71 LRG bin disagrees with the eBOSS LRG result at 2.5-3 sigma (about 3 sigma in DM/rd is an upper limit assuming a 0.21 cross-correlation; 2.7 sigma if uncorrelated); no non-statistical cause was found. Cosmological interpretation is deferred to the companion paper, and the low-z deviation to DESI Y3/Y5 data.

## Claims
- claim: The combined BAO precision across the six DR1 redshift bins is about 0.52%, a 1.2-fold improvement over the previous state of the art.
  quote: "the combined precision of the BAO measurements across the six different redshift bins is ∼0.52%, marking a 1.2-fold improvement over the previous state-of-the-art results using only first-year data"
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: BAO is detected in every bin, with significances ranging from 9.1 sigma (LRG3+ELG1) down to 4.0 sigma (BGS).
  quote: "The detection levels range from the highest 9.1σ for LRG3+ELG1 to the lowest 4.0σ for BGS."
  loc: "Sec. 9"
- claim: The measured BAO scales are systematically larger than the Planck 2018 LCDM prediction at z < 0.8, implying lower distances there.
  quote: "We find our measurements are systematically larger than the prediction of the Planck 2018-ΛCDM at z <0.8."
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: The analysis was blinded at the catalogue level, with the fiducial fitting and reconstruction setup and the systematic-error budget fixed before unblinding, while covariance calibration, an N_eff re-fit and catalogue fixes were allowed afterwards.
  quote: "To prevent this from affecting the DESI DR1 BAO measurements, we determined the systematic error budget detailed in Table 12 prior to unblinding."
  loc: "Sec. 6"
- claim: The total systematic error budget is 0.245% on the isotropic BAO scale and 0.300% on the anisotropic (Alcock-Paczynski) distortion.
  quote: "yielding conservative systematics estimates of 0.245% for the isotropic BAO scale and 0.300% for the anisotropic distortion of the BAO feature"
  loc: "Sec. 9"
- claim: The DESI LRG measurement at z_eff = 0.71 is discrepant with the SDSS eBOSS LRG measurement at z_eff = 0.7 at up to roughly 3 sigma in DM/rd (assuming a 0.21 cross-correlation; 2.7 sigma if the samples are uncorrelated).
  quote: "Accordingly, we found a large discrepancy between the DESI BAO measurement at zeff = 0.71 and the SDSS measurement at zeff = 0.7."
  loc: "Sec. 9"

## Methods
- Post-reconstruction correlation-function multipoles in configuration space are the fiducial measurement; Fourier-space fits are a cross-check.
- Density-field reconstruction using the RecSym convention, applied with a single unified pipeline to all galaxy and quasar tracers.
- BAO template fit with a new separation of BAO from broadband and a spline-based broadband model to reduce dependence on data signal-to-noise.
- Analytic covariance matrices (RascalC for the correlation function, thecov for the power spectrum) built on the unblinded DR1 data and calibrated by jackknife resampling; EZmock sample covariances used only to validate them, with agreement reached in configuration space but not yet in Fourier space.
- Systematic budget: the three detected terms (theory 0.1%/0.2%, HOD 0.2%, fiducial cosmology 0.1%) summed in quadrature to 0.245%/0.3%; observational, reconstruction and covariance effects tested on mocks and data and found negligible.

## Data
- DESI DR1 large-scale structure catalogues: 300,017 BGS galaxies (0.1 < z < 0.4), 2,138,600 LRGs (0.4 < z < 1.1), 2,432,022 ELGs (0.8 < z < 1.6), 856,652 quasars (0.8 < z < 2.1).
- About 7,500 square degrees of footprint and roughly 18 Gpc^3 effective volume, split into six redshift bins including a combined LRG+ELG tracer at 0.8 < z < 1.1.
- AbacusSummit and EZmock catalogues for pipeline validation and covariances; published BOSS/eBOSS catalogues reanalysed with the DESI pipeline.

## Limitations
- limit: The 2.5-3 sigma discrepancy with SDSS at z ~ 0.7 is unexplained; no non-statistical source was identified in either data set.
  quote: "While this 2.5 σ–3σ discrepancy warrants attention, we have not identified any sources of non-statistical discrepancy from both sets of the data."
  loc: "Sec. 8.2"
- limit: The adopted fiducial-cosmology systematic excludes the bias that an incorrect N_eff assumption in the fitting model could introduce.
  quote: "For the fiducial cosmology systematics, we do not include the bias that can be introduced due to an incorrect assumption of Neff in the fitting model."
  loc: "Table 13"
- limit: An error in the completeness weights assigned to random points was found only after unblinding, requiring post-unblinding catalogue corrections.
  quote: "After the unblinded results were revealed, an error was found in the LSS catalogs related to the completeness weights that are assigned to random points."
  loc: "Sec. 2.3"
- limit: The quoted 3 sigma SDSS-DESI tension depends on an assumed cross-correlation between the two samples, so it is only an upper limit on the significance.
  quote: "Hence, the cross-correlation coefficient could be much lower than the theoretical estimate, suggesting that the observed 3σ discrepancy between SDSS and DESI is likely an upper limit."
  loc: "Sec. 8.2"

## Open questions
- What is the physical or statistical origin of the systematically larger BAO scales at z < 0.8 relative to Planck LCDM, and will it persist in DESI Y3 and Y5 data? (Sec. 8.2, Sec. 9)
- What are the cosmological implications of the low-redshift deviations when combined with the Lyman-alpha BAO measurement? Deferred to the companion cosmology paper. (Sec. 8.1, Sec. 9)
- Does the improved DESI pipeline (RecSym reconstruction, spline broadband) give a measurable advantage over the SDSS methodology once statistical precision increases? (Sec. 9)
- How much further can combined-tracer catalogues (LRG+ELG) improve precision in future DESI analyses beyond the ~10% gain seen in DR1? (Sec. 7.5, Sec. 9)
