# Executive summary

This is the final Planck Collaboration paper on cosmological parameters. It asks how well six-parameter flat ΛCDM describes the full-mission CMB temperature, polarization and lensing data, and whether any common extension is preferred. The data are the 2018 HFI/LFI maps, with a new low-multipole polarization likelihood that pins the reionization optical depth (τ = 0.054 ± 0.007) and two high-multipole TT,TE,EE likelihoods (Plik, CamSpec) built from the same Planck maps but with different corrections for polarization systematics; their differences bound residual systematics at the ≲0.5σ level. Parameters are sampled with CosmoMC using CAMB spectra, and the CMB is combined with galaxy BAO (6dFGS, SDSS-MGS, BOSS DR12), Pantheon supernovae, RSD, DES lensing and BBN. The headline result is that base ΛCDM fits everything well: H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.315 ± 0.007, σ8 = 0.811 ± 0.006, ns = 0.965 ± 0.004, and with BAO the Universe is flat to ΩK = 0.0007 ± 0.0019, w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03 with SNe, Σmν < 0.12 eV and Neff = 2.99 ± 0.17. No extension is favoured. What remains open are the tensions: a 4.4σ gap with distance-ladder H0, a Lyα BAO offset at z ≈ 2.4 that was about 2.3σ for the superseded SDSS DR12 data (the conclusions quote "about 2.5σ" without naming a release) but 1.7σ for the combined DR14 DM/rdrag value in Sec. 5.1, a 2.5σ lower S8 from DES clustering, and an internal preference for more lensing smoothing (AL > 1) that the lensing reconstruction does not support (its curvature counterpart BAO pulls back to flat). The paper leaves unresolved whether these are systematics, fluctuations or new physics.
## Claims
- claim: Base ΛCDM fitted to Planck requires H0 = 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc, which is 4.4σ below the Riess et al. (2019) distance-ladder value, and none of the studied extensions convincingly removes the gap.
  quote: "The Planck base-ΛCDM cosmology requires a Hubble constant H0 = (67.4± 0.5) km s−1Mpc−1, in substantial 4.4σ tension with the latest local determination by Riess et al. (2019)."
  loc: "Sec. 8, p. 62"
- claim: Combining Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing with galaxy BAO breaks the geometric degeneracy and gives ΩK = 0.0007 ± 0.0019, i.e. a flat Universe to 0.2 % at 1σ.
  quote: "The joint results suggests our Universe is spatially ﬂat to a 1 σ accuracy of 0.2 %."
  loc: "Sec. 7.3, p. 42"
- claim: With Pantheon supernovae and BAO added, a constant dark-energy equation of state is w0 = −1.03 ± 0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant, and the (w0, wa) fit improves χ² by only 1.4 for two extra parameters.
  quote: "Combining Planck data with Pantheon supernovae and BAO data, the equation of state of dark energy is tightly constrained to w0 =−1.03± 0.03, consistent with a cosmological constant."
  loc: "Sec. 8, p. 62"
- claim: Planck plus BAO bounds the sum of neutrino masses to Σmν < 0.12 eV at 95 % for degenerate active neutrinos.
  quote: "Allowing for a free degenerate active neutrino mass, and combining with BAO measurements, we obtain the tight 95 % constraint on the sum of the masses"
  loc: "Sec. 8, p. 62"
- claim: Galaxy BAO measurements are treated as the primary non-CMB data set because the 147 Mpc acoustic scale is far above virialized scales, making BAO a geometric test insensitive to nonlinear physics.
  quote: "This separation of scales makes BAO measurements insensitive to nonlinear physics, providing a robust geometrical test of cosmology."
  loc: "Sec. 5.1, p. 22"
- claim: In the updated Sec. 5.1 the combined DM/rdrag value from the SDSS DR14 Lyα analyses (de Sainte Agathe et al. 2019; Blomqvist et al. 2019, as quoted by the latter) lies within 1.7σ of the Planck best fit, whereas the superseded DR12 Lyα result of Bautista et al. (2017) and du Mas des Bourboux et al. (2017) was about 2.3σ low; the conclusions separately cite "some tension (at about 2.5σ)" with quasar Lyα BAO without naming a release, and state that no standard ΛCDM extension improves the fit.
  quote: "The combined result for DM/rdrag from these analyses (as quoted by Blomqvist et al. 2019) is plotted as the orange hexagon on Fig. 11 and lies within 1 .7σ of the Planck best-ﬁt model."
  loc: "Sec. 5.1, p. 23"

## Methods
- Six-parameter flat ΛCDM baseline (ωb, ωc, θMC, τ, As, ns) with one 0.06 eV massive neutrino; flat priors; lensed spectra from CAMB (Aug 2017) and MCMC sampling with CosmoMC (Jul 2018), cross-checked against CLASS/MontePython.
- Hybrid likelihood: low-ℓ (ℓ < 30) temperature and SRoll-based HFI polarization likelihood, plus Gaussian pseudo-cross-spectrum likelihoods at high ℓ; two high-ℓ TT,TE,EE likelihoods (Plik baseline, CamSpec) with different polarization-systematics corrections, used to bound the resulting uncertainty at ≲0.5σ (0.7σ for AL, 0.5σ for ΩK).
- CMB lensing reconstruction likelihood (Planck 2018 VIII) added to power spectra; nonlinear corrections via HMcode in halofit; helium fraction from PArthENoPE BBN interpolation.
- Grid of one- and two-parameter extensions (ΩK, w0/wa via PPF, Neff, Σmν, AL, r, running, modified gravity via MGCAMB/EFTCAMB), each run with and without external data.
- Consistency tests: AL lensing-amplitude parameter, low vs high multipole splits, TT vs TE vs EE, and comparison of inferred parameters with BAO, SNe, RSD, DES, BBN and local H0.

## Data
- Planck 2018 full-mission HFI (SRoll maps, dropping the last 1000 scanning rings) and LFI maps; TT, TE, EE power spectra and the CMB lensing potential spectrum.
- BAO: 6dFGS, SDSS-MGS DV/rdrag and BOSS DR12 anisotropic DM(z), H(z) at z = 0.38, 0.51, 0.61; eBOSS quasar and BOSS DR14 Lyα BAO shown for comparison but excluded from the default compilation.
- Pantheon Type Ia supernovae (0.01 < z < 2.3), BOSS DR12 RSD fσ8, DES Y1 weak lensing and clustering, BICEP2/Keck 2015 B-modes, and primordial deuterium/helium abundances.
- Local H0 from SH0ES (Riess et al. 2018a/2019), used only for comparison, not combined.

## Limitations
- limit: The high-multipole TT,TE,EE likelihoods still carry imperfectly modelled polarization systematics; the authors claim accuracy only to within the quoted error bars.
  quote: "Although the TT,TE,EE likelihoods are not perfect, thePlanck parameter results presented in this paper can be considered accurate to within their error bars."
  loc: "Sec. 8, p. 61"
- limit: The CMB power spectra alone prefer more lensing smoothing than ΛCDM predicts (AL > 1 at 2.8σ), an internal tension the paper flags alongside the external ones.
  quote: "The joint combined likelihood shifts the value preferred by the TT data downwards towards AL = 1, but the error also shrinks, increasing the signiﬁcance of AL > 1 to 2.8σ"
  loc: "Sec. 6.2, p. 35"
- limit: The curvature and AL results from the joint polarization likelihood are not robust to the choice of high-ℓ likelihood: Plik and CamSpec differ by 0.5σ for ΩK and 0.7σ for AL (Sec. 2.2.5, p. 12).
  quote: "As with the AL > 1 preference, the joint Planck polarization result is not robust at the approximately 0.5σ level to modelling of the polarization likelihoods"
  loc: "Sec. 7.3, p. 41"
- limit: Lyα BAO at z ≈ 2.4 are left out of the default BAO compilation because their errors are large relative to galaxy BAO, so the quoted combined constraints do not include the highest-redshift distance data.
  quote: "The errors on these high-redshift BAO measurements are still quite large in comparison with the galaxy measurements and so we do not include them in our default BAO compilation."
  loc: "Sec. 5.1, p. 23"

## Open questions
- Whether the residual discrepancy between Planck and Lyα BAO (2.3σ for DR12, 1.7σ for the DR14 combined DM/rdrag value) is a statistical fluctuation, a small systematic, or new physics, since no well-motivated extension resolves it (Sec. 5.1, p. 23–24).
- Whether the 4.4σ H0 tension reflects systematics in Planck or SH0ES, or genuinely new physics, given that neither extra Neff nor evolving dark energy convincingly resolves it (Sec. 5.4; Sec. 8).
- Whether the internal AL > 1 preference and the DES S8 offset are small Planck systematics, external systematics or hints beyond ΛCDM (Sec. 6.2; Sec. 8).
- What the next decade of large BAO and weak-lensing surveys, 21-cm and gravitational-wave experiments will reveal, since any new-physics signature in the CMB itself must be small (Sec. 8, p. 62).
