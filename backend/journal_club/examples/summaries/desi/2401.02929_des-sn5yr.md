# Executive summary
The paper asks what the full five-year Dark Energy Survey supernova programme says about the expansion history, and in particular whether dark energy behaves like a cosmological constant. The sample is 1635 DES Type Ia supernovae at 0.10 < z < 1.13, classified photometrically with machine-learning light-curve classifiers (SuperNNova and SCONE) rather than by spectroscopy, with host-galaxy spectroscopic redshifts mainly from OzDES, plus a deliberately small external low-redshift anchor of 194 SNe (CfA3/4, CSP, Foundation) at 0.025 < z < 0.10. Distances come from SALT3 fits with BBC bias corrections and a BEAMS treatment that folds classification probabilities into the Hubble diagram; the whole pipeline was blinded and validated on simulations before unblinding. Supernovae alone give Omega_M = 0.352 +/- 0.017 in flat LCDM, acceleration (q0 < 0 in non-flat LCDM) at 5.2 sigma (overwhelming once flatness is assumed), w = -0.80 (+0.14/-0.16) in flat wCDM, and (w0, wa) = (-0.36, -8.8) in flat w0waCDM, about 2 sigma from (-1, 0) in the "thawing" direction. Adding Planck, SDSS BAO and DES 3x2pt gives w = -0.941 +/- 0.026. Systematic errors are subdominant to statistics and contamination from misclassification is shown not to be a limiting systematic. The paper concludes that LCDM remains a good fit, with a mild, inconclusive preference for w > -1 and evolving dark energy, and leaves open whether that preference survives a better-calibrated low-z sample and improved intrinsic-scatter modelling.

## Claims
- claim: Using supernovae alone, DES-SN5YR measures Omega_M = 0.352 +/- 0.017 in flat LCDM, the tightest supernova-only constraint to date.
  quote: "Using supernova data alone and including systematic uncertainties we find Ω M = 0 .352 ± 0.017 in flat-ΛCDM"
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: The DES sample quintuples the number of high-quality z > 0.5 SNe relative to Pantheon+.
  quote: "This quintuples the number of high-quality z > 0.5 SNe compared to the previous leading compilation of Pantheon+, and results in the tightest cosmological constraints achieved by any supernova data set to date"
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: Supernova data on their own now require cosmic acceleration (q0 < 0 in LCDM) at more than 5 sigma.
  quote: "Supernova data alone now require acceleration ( q0 < 0 in ΛCDM) with over 5 σ confidence"
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: In flat w0waCDM, DES-SN5YR alone prefers a time-varying, thawing dark energy at slightly over 2 sigma from a cosmological constant, with (Omega_M, w0, wa) = (0.495, -0.36, -8.8).
  quote: "Fitting DES-SN5YR alone to the Flat- w0waCDM model gives an equation of state that is slightly over 2σ from a cosmological constant, marginally preferring a time-varying dark energy"
  loc: "Sec. 4.1.4"
- claim: The best-fit constant equation of state is more than 1 sigma above w = -1, both for SNe alone and when combined with Planck or BAO+3x2pt.
  quote: "Our best fit equation of state parameter is slightly (more than 1 σ) higher than the cosmological constant value of w = −1"
  loc: "Sec. 5.1.2"
- claim: Photometric classification and host-galaxy matching contamination are demonstrated not to be a limiting systematic for SN cosmology, with systematics subdominant to statistical errors.
  quote: "Critically, the DES-SN5YR analysis shown here demonstrates that contamination due to SN classification and host-galaxy matching is not a limiting systematic for SN cosmology"
  loc: "Sec. 6"

## Methods
- Machine-learning photometric classification (SuperNNova and SCONE) with per-SN Type Ia probabilities fed into the BEAMS formalism, instead of spectroscopic confirmation; contamination estimated at ~6.5%.
- SALT3 light-curve fits converted to distance moduli via a Tripp relation with a host-galaxy step, with BBC bias corrections from large simulations and a dust-based (BS21) intrinsic-scatter model.
- Full statistical+systematic covariance (calibration, intrinsic scatter, redshift corrections) following Conley et al. (2011); fits via MCMC in CosmoSIS (emcee, PolyChord).
- Blinded analysis with explicit unblinding criteria: simulation/data agreement, recovery of input cosmology on 25 mock samples (bias on w of 0.001 +/- 0.020), contour validation, independence of reference cosmology.
- Tension and model selection assessed with Suspiciousness (prior-independent) and Bayesian evidence on Jeffreys' scale.

## Data
- 1635 DES SNe at 0.10 < z < 1.13 from five seasons (2013-2018) of ten ~3 deg^2 fields in griz, with host spectroscopic redshifts primarily from OzDES; 1499 have P(Ia) > 0.5.
- 194 external low-z SNe Ia (CfA3, CfA4, CSP DR3, Foundation) at 0.025 < z < 0.10, chosen for calibration homogeneity; total Hubble-diagram sample 1829 SNe.
- External probes: Planck Collaboration (2020) CMB TTTEEE spectra, evaluated with the Python implementation of Planck's 2015 Plik-lite likelihood (Prince & Dunkley 2019) (labelled "Planck 2020" in the paper's tables and figures), DES Y3 3x2pt (MagLim), and SDSS/eBOSS BAO-only measurements (MGS, BOSS, eBOSS LRG/ELG/QSO/Lya).
- Hubble diagram, covariance, chains and code released publicly (github.com/des-science/DES-SN5YR).

## Limitations
- limit: The external low-redshift sample is the part of the dataset over which the analysis has the least systematic control.
  quote: "it is important to recognize that (a) the low- z sample is the one for which we have the least systematic control"
  loc: "Sec. 5.1.2"
- limit: The highest-redshift SNe carry large (> 0.1 mag) and more uncertain bias corrections.
  quote: "the very high-redshift SNe are the ones for which bias-corrections are large ( > 0.1 mag) and more uncertain"
  loc: "Sec. 5.1.2"
- limit: The intrinsic-scatter (dust) model remains the largest simulation-driven systematic uncertainty.
  quote: "this intrinsic scatter model remains the largest source of systematic uncertainty from the simulation"
  loc: "Sec. 2.2"
- limit: SN-only w-Omega_M contours are strongly non-Gaussian, so quoted best-fit values are an insufficient summary and can shift substantially along the degeneracy.
  quote: "The best fit value for w or Ω M is thus an insufficient summary of the SN information, as a small shift along the degeneracy direction can result in large shifts in the best-fit values"
  loc: "Sec. 4.1.3"

## Open questions
- Whether a new, homogeneous low-z sample (ZTF, YSE, DEBASS) removes the remaining calibration doubt and the slight w > -1 pull in Flat-wCDM, which weakens when the low-z sample is dropped (SN-only best fit moves to w = -1.34 +/- 0.32, a Delta w = 0.54 shift that 100 Cholesky realizations put at 2.3 sigma, mostly but not entirely along the degeneracy line; with a CMB-like prior the shift is below 2 sigma, and the Flat-w0waCDM result is stable to sub-sample selection) (Sec. 5.1.2, Sec. 5.3, App. C, Table 4).
- Whether the ~2400 DES SNe without host spectroscopic redshifts can be added using photometric redshifts without introducing systematics comparable to the statistics (Sec. 5.3).
- How to treat second-order correlations between systematics (calibration, light-curve model, intrinsic scatter) jointly, e.g. via forward modelling / ABC, as samples grow (Sec. 5.3).
- Whether stellar-age constraints should be used as a prior to limit time-varying dark energy, given the best-fit w0waCDM age is ~9% younger than Planck LCDM (Sec. 5.1.3).
