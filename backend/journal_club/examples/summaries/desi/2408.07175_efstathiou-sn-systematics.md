# Executive summary
The paper asks whether the DESI Year-1 evidence for evolving dark energy is real or an artefact of the Type Ia supernova (SN) compilation paired with BAO and CMB data. DESI's significance ranges from 2.5 sigma with Pantheon+ to 3.9 sigma with DES5Y, so the author fits each catalogue alone (1417 Pantheon+ and 1754 DES5Y SN at 0.02 <= zHD <= 1.2) to Planck LCDM, LCDM with free Omega_m, and a w0-wa model. Pantheon+ alone is consistent with Planck LCDM and gains nothing from a time-varying equation of state; DES5Y alone disfavours Planck LCDM at roughly 3 sigma (delta chi-squared 12.5; about 2 sigma over its own high-Omega_m LCDM fit) and wants high Omega_m, w0 > -1 and strongly negative wa. For SN common to both catalogues, the DES5Y zero point is set by the Planck LCDM fit to Pantheon+, and only the low-to-high-redshift differential matters: the 145 common DES SN sit near zero (-0.012 mag), while the 118 common Foundation SN (-0.051 mag) and most other low-redshift samples are offset by about 0.04-0.05 mag. Adding these per-survey offsets back to the DES5Y low-redshift subsamples removes its preference for evolving dark energy (delta chi-squared of only 3 for w0-wa) and restores agreement with Pantheon+ and Planck. The offsets correlate strongly with the simulation-based bias corrections applied in DES5Y, which are large (about -0.047 mag) for the low-redshift SN but small in Pantheon+ for the same objects, so the author attributes the 0.04 mag shift to the DES5Y bias corrections and concludes that the evidence for evolving dark energy is most likely a result of systematics in the DES5Y sample. He calls for a detailed intercomparison of the Pantheon+, Union3 and DES5Y catalogues before SN data are used to claim new physics.

## Claims
- claim: The strength of the DESI evidence for evolving dark energy depends strongly on the SN sample, ranging from about 3.9 sigma with DES5Y to about 2.5 sigma with Pantheon+.
  quote: "The strength of the evidence for evolving dark energy ranges from ∼ 3.9σ for the Dark Energy 5 year (DES5Y) SN sample to ∼ 2.5σ for the Pantheon+ sample."
  loc: "Abstract"
- claim: The Pantheon+ SN sample on its own is consistent with the Planck LCDM model and shows no preference for a time-varying equation of state.
  quote: "There is no improvement in χ2 compared to ΛCDM if w0 and wa are allowed to vary. The Pantheon+ SN data therefore offer no support for models with a time varying equation-of-state."
  loc: "Sec. 2"
- claim: DES5Y alone prefers evolving dark energy, improving chi-squared by 12.5 relative to the Planck LCDM fit (disfavouring it at roughly 3 sigma) but only by 7.4 relative to DES5Y's own high-Omega_m LCDM fit, about 2 sigma.
  quote: "The DES5Y compilation clearly prefers evolving dark energy, with ∆χ2 = 12.5 for the best fit Planck ΛCDM cosmology and ∆χ2 = 7.4 for the best fit DES5Y ΛCDM cosmology."
  loc: "Sec. 2"
- claim: SN common to both catalogues reveal that most low-redshift surveys in DES5Y are offset by about 0.04-0.05 mag relative to the more distant DES SN.
  quote: "Most of the low redshift surveys are offset by ∼ 0.04−0.05 mag. relative to the more distant DES supernovae."
  loc: "Sec. 3"
- claim: Correcting the DES5Y low-redshift subsamples for the measured offsets removes the statistically significant preference for evolving dark energy.
  quote: "Evidently, the recalibrated DES5Y catalogue does not show a statistically significant preference for a time varying equation-of-state."
  loc: "Sec. 3"
- claim: The low-to-high-redshift offset is attributed to the simulation-based bias corrections in the DES5Y catalogue, which are large for the low-redshift SN and correlate strongly with the measured magnitude offsets.
  quote: "However, for the low redshift SN, the mean bias corrections in the DES5Y catalogue are large and correlate strongly with the mean magnitude offsets presented in the previous section."
  loc: "Sec. 4"

## Methods
- Fit the unbinned Pantheon+ and DES5Y Hubble diagrams, using full covariance matrices, to Planck LCDM, to LCDM with Omega_m free (Pantheon+ 0.333 +/- 0.018, delta chi-squared 1.2; DES5Y 0.354 +/- 0.017, delta chi-squared 5.1), and to the w0-wa (CPL) parameterisation; binned residuals are shown for display only.
- Convert DES5Y distance moduli to the Pantheon+ magnitude system with a constant offset (mDES = muDES - 19.33) fixed by the Planck LCDM fit to Pantheon+.
- Compare magnitudes of SN present in both catalogues (145 DES Y3 SN, 118 Foundation SN, plus CfA and CSP samples), grouped by source survey.
- Recompute parameter posteriors with MULTINEST after adding the per-survey offsets of Fig. 4 to the DES5Y magnitudes, i.e. recalibrating DES5Y to match Pantheon+.
- Tabulate the mean bias corrections applied to each common subsample in both catalogues (Table 1) and plot the magnitude offset against the mean DES5Y bias correction (Fig. 7).

## Data
- Pantheon+ SN catalogue (Scolnic et al. 2022): 1417 SN retained with 0.02 <= zHD <= 1.2, out of 1701 entries.
- DES5Y SN catalogue (DES Collaboration 2024): 1754 SN retained after the same redshift cut and removal of SN with magnitude errors above 1, out of 1829.
- Planck TTTEEE LCDM best fit from the 12.5cln chains of Efstathiou & Gratton (2021), Omega_m = 0.3135 +/- 0.0081, as the reference cosmology.

## Limitations
- limit: The author cannot independently assess the fidelity of the simulation-heavy standardisation pipelines and only raises the possibility that the bias corrections are in error, without demonstrating it.
  quote: "This raises the possibility that systematic errors in the bias corrections are responsible for the differences in the cosmological results from Pantheon+ and DES5Y."
  loc: "Sec. 4"
- limit: The Union3 compilation, which also gave high significance in DESI24, could not be analysed because its individual SN are not public.
  quote: "The Union3 compilation for individual SN is not yet in the public domain and would, in any case, require a very different type of analysis to that presented here."
  loc: "Sec. 1"
- limit: The SN standardisation methodologies are complicated and simulation-dependent, so a non-expert cannot verify which catalogue is correct.
  quote: "The methodologies that have been developed to achieve standardization are complicated and heavily dependent on simulations. It is therefore very difficult for a non-expert to assess the fidelity of these catalogues."
  loc: "Sec. 5"
- limit: Even after the offset correction, the DES5Y constraints remain skewed to high Omega_m because of a negative residual at z > 1.
  quote: "The corrected DESY5 constraints are still skewed to high values of Ω m as a consequence of the negative residual at z >1 in Fig. 5"
  loc: "Sec. 3"

## Open questions
- Why do the bias corrections for the heavily overlapping low-redshift SN samples differ by about 0.04 mag between Pantheon+ and DES5Y, and which catalogue's corrections are in error? (Sec. 4)
- Does the Union3 compilation, which gives 3.5 sigma evidence in DESI24, suffer from a similar low-to-high-redshift calibration difference? (Sec. 1 and Sec. 5)
- What causes the negative residual at z > 1 that keeps the corrected DES5Y constraints skewed to high Omega_m? (Sec. 3)
- Would a detailed intercomparison of the Pantheon+, Union3 and DES5Y catalogues confirm that the DESI24 preference for dynamical dark energy is driven by SN systematics? (Sec. 5)
